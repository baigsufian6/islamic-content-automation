# Islamic Video — Professional Design Specification

## 1. Design Objective

The final video must feel:

- peaceful
- reverent
- cinematic
- sophisticated
- emotionally powerful
- uncluttered
- professional
- respectful

It must NOT look like a generic AI-generated slideshow.

---

# 2. Visual Identity

The visual identity should be consistent across the channel.

Use:

- cinematic composition
- subtle movement
- elegant typography
- restrained transitions
- atmospheric lighting
- carefully selected backgrounds
- consistent spacing
- consistent typography hierarchy

---

# 3. Primary Layout

The vertical video uses:

1080 × 1920

The design should contain three primary information zones.

### TOP

Small, elegant verse reference:

SURAH 22 • AL-HAJJ • AYAH 13

### CENTER

Arabic Qur'an text.

### LOWER CENTER

English translation.

The cinematic visual remains visible behind the typography.

---

# 4. Qur'an Reference

The reference must always be visible.

Format:

SURAH {NUMBER} • {SURAH NAME} • AYAH {NUMBER}

Example:

SURAH 22 • AL-HAJJ • AYAH 13

The reference must change exactly when the displayed ayah changes.

---

# 5. Arabic Typography

Arabic is the visual priority.

The Arabic text must be:

- large
- elegant
- highly readable
- correctly connected
- correctly right-to-left
- correctly rendered
- appropriately spaced

The selected Arabic font must support the required Qur'anic characters
and marks.

---

# 6. English Translation

The English translation appears on the SAME FRAME as the Arabic.

It must never be placed on a separate slide.

English should be:

- smaller than Arabic
- highly readable
- professionally spaced
- centered or carefully aligned
- visually subordinate to the Arabic

---

# 7. Long Ayahs

Long ayahs must not be forced into tiny text.

The renderer must support:

- multiple lines
- controlled line spacing
- dynamic text sizing within safe limits
- intelligent text layout

If an ayah cannot be displayed clearly within the available area, the
system must flag it for review instead of making the text unreadably small.

---

# 8. Background Visuals

Backgrounds may include:

- Qur'an manuscripts
- Qur'an calligraphy
- mosques
- mosque interiors
- Islamic architecture
- arches
- courtyards
- lanterns
- Islamic geometric patterns
- historical architecture
- deserts
- mountains
- oceans
- rain
- clouds
- stars
- sky
- water
- atmospheric environments
- abstract cinematic environments
- slow-motion footage

Nature is optional.

---

# 9. Human Imagery

Default:

NO PEOPLE.

Never use:

- visible faces
- identifiable people
- prophets
- companions
- angels depicted as humans
- religious personalities

Faceless human imagery should not be used unless specifically approved
for a particular visual concept.

Default production remains:

NO PEOPLE.

---

# 10. Visual Meaning

Visual selection must consider the meaning and emotional tone of the
ayah.

Examples:

Mercy / forgiveness:

- rain
- dawn
- gentle light
- flowing water
- peaceful architecture

Warning:

- storms
- dark clouds
- harsh landscapes
- dramatic atmospheric conditions

Creation:

- mountains
- stars
- oceans
- skies
- natural phenomena

Reflection:

- Qur'an
- architecture
- quiet interiors
- night sky
- subtle atmospheric scenes

The system must never claim that generated imagery is a literal depiction
of unseen realities.

---

# 11. Visual Movement

Movement must be slow and cinematic.

Preferred:

- slow camera movement
- slow push-in
- slow pull-out
- subtle parallax
- gentle environmental movement
- slow clouds
- rain
- water movement
- subtle light movement

Avoid:

- rapid zooms
- spinning
- flashy transitions
- excessive particles
- aggressive camera movement
- generic AI effects

---

# 12. Text Background Treatment

Text must remain readable without destroying the background.

Possible techniques:

- subtle dark gradient behind text
- soft vignette
- controlled blur
- subtle shadow
- restrained translucent panel
- localized contrast

The background should remain visible.

---

# 13. Text Hierarchy

Priority:

1. Arabic Qur'an
2. English translation
3. Surah/Ayah reference

The viewer should immediately recognize the Arabic as the primary content.

---

# 14. Safe Areas

Keep all critical text away from:

- top platform controls
- bottom platform controls
- screen edges
- left/right cropping zones

The design must remain readable on phones.

---

# 15. Transitions Between Ayahs

When moving from one ayah to another:

- old Arabic disappears
- old translation disappears
- old reference disappears
- new reference appears
- new Arabic appears
- new translation appears

The transition must be subtle.

Preferred:

- gentle fade
- controlled crossfade
- subtle motion

Avoid flashy transitions.

---

# 16. Background Continuity

The background may:

A. Continue naturally between ayahs

OR

B. Change to a new visually appropriate scene.

If the background changes, the transition must be smooth.

---

# 17. Emotional Tone

The design must support the message without manipulating the viewer
through excessive effects.

The Qur'an itself remains the focus.

---

# 18. Branding

Branding must remain subtle.

Do not place a large logo over the Qur'an.

If channel branding is added later, it should be:

- small
- unobtrusive
- consistent
- positioned safely

---

# 19. Watermarks

No unwanted third-party watermarks.

No stock footage watermarks.

No AI-generation watermarks.

Only properly licensed/owned assets may be used.

---

# 20. Professional Standard

The final viewer should feel:

"This is a carefully produced Islamic media video."

They should NOT feel:

"This is an automatically generated template."

---

# 21. Quality Priority

The priority order is:

Qur'an accuracy
>
Readability
>
Respect
>
Visual quality
>
Animation
>
Automation speed

---

# 22. Core Rule

Beautiful is good.

Meaningful is better.

Accurate is essential.
